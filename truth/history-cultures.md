## 2. Lu Yu's boiling stages
- Lu Yu (Tang dynasty) wrote the Cha Jing (Classic of Tea) between 760 and 762 CE. The original manuscript is lost. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Classic_of_Tea
- The exact English wording, from Okakura's *The Book of Tea* (1906), ch. II, which calls him "Lu Wu": "There are three stages of boiling: the first boil is when the little bubbles like the eye of fishes swim on the surface; the second boil is when the bubbles are like crystal beads rolling in a fountain; the third boil is when the billows surge wildly in the kettle." He ranks water too: "the mountain spring is the best." https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Tea/Chapter_II
  - Note: our facts.md uses "fish eyes, string of pearls, rolling waves". That is a common modern paraphrase. Okakura's wording is "eye of fishes, crystal beads, billows".
- Matsukaze (松風, "pine wind") is the quiet whistle of the chanoyu iron kettle at its last boiling stage, when the water is ideal for tea. It "resembles that of a breeze passing through a grove of pine trees". A thin iron piece at the kettle's base makes it whistle at about 190 °F (~88 °C). A Zen scroll, *Shōfū satsu satsu no koe*, asks you to listen quietly to it. https://www.seattlejapanesegarden.org/blog/2023/2/3/japanese-tea-and-the-art-of-listening (a garden blog, not an academic source)

## 3. Te and cha
- There are two word families. "Te" comes from Min Chinese (the Fujian ports of Xiamen and Quanzhou) and spread by sea. "Cha" comes from Cantonese and Mandarin and spread over land to Central Asia, South Asia and the Middle East. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_tea
- Dutch "thee" comes from Min Chinese. Through the Dutch East India Company it spread to English, French, German and Spanish. Portuguese "chá" came from Cantonese via Macau in the 1550s, the one sea-route exception. Same.
- Te: thee, thé, Tee, té, teh. Cha: chá, chay (Persian), чай (Russian), chiyā (Nepali). English took both: "char" in the late 1500s via Portugal, "tea" in the 1600s via the Dutch. Same.
- Linnaeus's *Species Plantarum* (1753) named the plant *Thea sinensis*, in its own genus *Thea*. Today it is *Camellia sinensis*. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camellia_sinensis
- The Dutch angle may suit a Dutch jury: the Dutch carried "tea" to the West.

## 4. The tea bag
- Thomas Sullivan, a New York tea and coffee importer, marketed silk tea bags "in about 1908". The story that it was an accident is "a popular legend". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_bag
- He was not first. Roberta Lawson and Mary McLaren filed in 1901 and were granted US patent 723287 ("Tea Leaf Holder") in 1903. Same.
- UK today:
  - "Over 96 per cent of tea in Britain is now made with teabags." Teabags let makers use the lowest grades of tea. QMUL, 2016: https://www.qmul.ac.uk/news/latest-news/2016/hss/why-britain-is-losing-its-taste-for-tea--and-how-hipsters-could-revive-it.html
  - The UK Tea & Infusions Association says 97.5% of British tea is drunk from a teabag (undated, no source given): https://www.tea.co.uk/tea-faqs
- Safe slide line: "More than 96% of Britain's tea now comes from a bag."
- No sourced figure for "the West" overall. Don't claim one.

## 5. Aroma (supports the name OSME)
- Temperature shapes the scent. Green tea brewed hotter gives "a stronger roasted note", while "a lower-temperature extraction resulted in a stronger floral note". Lin et al. 2021, *Food Science & Nutrition*, citing Tao & Liu 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7866617
- Hot water frees aroma. In black tea, "the hot water treatment led to an increase of, in particular, most of the alcohols, i.e. geraniol" (a floral, rose-like odorant). Schieberle & Schuh 2006: https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/aroma-compounds-in-black-tea-powders-of-different-origins-changes/
- Story line: the wrong temperature changes what your tea smells like. The right one opens its scent. That is OSME.
- GAP: I found no study on how fast tea loses aroma after the pack is opened. Storage studies (green tea, Keemun) show floral volatiles fading over months, but I could not open those pages (PMC verification wall), so they are not cited. Do not put a number on aroma loss.

## 6. Why now
- Matcha boom: Japan's green tea exports reached 13,125 t in FY2025, up 42%, and their value rose 2.2-fold to ¥84.7 bn. Powdered tea, including matcha, is about 70% of export volume. https://english.adnkronos.com/2026/05/01/japan-green-tea-exports-jump-in-fy-2025-amid-global-matcha-boom/
- Specialty tea: tea businesses rank premium and specialty loose-leaf the #2 trending product (15%), behind functional and wellness teas (25.1%). 58% expect moderate growth. https://www.worldteanews.com/innovation/results-2025-state-tea-industry-survey
- Young people use tea as a ritual: 62% of Gen Z have made a cup of tea to prepare themselves mentally for something, against 40% of Boomers. 49% say a cup before a challenge makes them feel like "the main character in their own life". https://twinings.co.uk/blogs/news/gentea-gen-z-turns-to-tea (Twinings marketing research; no sample size or date given)
- The material exists. PNIPAm-grafted membranes act as thermal gates: below the LCST of 32 °C the chains swell and "closed off the gates"; above it they shrink and the pores open. The switch repeated cleanly over 5 cycles (40 °C / 25 °C). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9920928/
  - CAVEAT: plain PNIPAm switches at 32 °C, far below brewing temperatures (70-100 °C). This paper does not cover tuning it higher. The OSME bag needs a switch point per tea, so frame it as "proven principle, tuned per tea": inspired by, not off the shelf.
