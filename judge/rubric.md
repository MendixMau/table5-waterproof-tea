# Judge rubric: Table 5, "The Art of Tea"

The jury is presales and sales professionals. They score like buyers. Score each criterion 1 to 5 at
Judge pass 1 (12:00) and pass 2 (12:45). Anything under 4 at pass 2 gets one named fix and an owner.
Ambiguity rule from `checklists.md` applies: if you cannot tell whether it is a 3 or a 4, write the question down.

## Scale

1 missing · 2 present but weak · 3 adequate, a juror would not remember it · 4 strong, one gap · 5 as described below.

## Criteria

| # | Criterion | What a 5 looks like | What drags it to 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clarity | After slide 1 a juror can say in one sentence what the bag does and who buys it. The one-liner is under 10 words and names a tea bag. | "Art of Tea" with no product noun; the jury has to wait for slide 3 to learn what it is. |
| 2 | Customer evidence | One named customer type, 3 or more sources where they were found (reviews, forums, buyer posts), at least one quote verbatim with a link, and a number on how many of them exist. | "Tea lovers" or "everyone"; sources are vendor pages; quotes nobody can find. |
| 3 | Credibility of the product | The mechanism is tied to something that exists today (temperature-responsive materials, named, with a source), the limits are said out loud, and the price covers a believable cost. | Magic material, no source, price picked to sound nice. |
| 4 | Demo | Product is already open. In under 60 seconds: pick a tea, water hits the ideal temperature, the bag opens, the cup colours, water cools, the bag closes. Same values as the slides. Works offline. | Demo starts from a login screen, breaks, or runs a temperature that contradicts the deck. |
| 5 | Story | Tea history to the tea bag trade-off (convenience beat craft) to "technology brings the craft back", told in three beats, every slide following from the last. The history is sourced, not folklore. | History lecture that eats a minute; story and business slides feel like two different pitches. |
| 6 | The ask | One concrete ask a sales jury can say yes to: an amount for what, a pilot with whom, or a named intro, with what happens next. | "Your support", or three asks at once. |
| 7 | Business and market (bonus, often scored) | Market size with working on the slide (bottom-up: buyers x price x frequency), margin per box, one route to market. | A top-down "tea is a $X bn market" with no path to it. |

Total out of 30 for criteria 1 to 6. Target at pass 2: 24 or more, no criterion below 3.

## The 5 hardest questions a judge will ask Table 5

Rehearse these. Each answer at most 20 seconds, and it must use numbers from `facts.md` only.

1. **"Does this material exist, or did you invent it?"**
   Suggested: name the real basis and its status. Thermo-responsive polymers (for example materials that
   change permeability around a set temperature) exist in labs and medical use; a food-safe tea bag is our
   R&D step. Say what is proven and what is not. Then: "The ask funds exactly that proof." Do not claim it works today.

2. **"Why would anyone pay more than for a normal tea bag? I just take the bag out."**
   Suggested: the buyer is not the casual drinker but the facts.md primary customer, who pays for a perfect cup
   every time without a timer (the self-stopping steep) and two to three infusions per bag, so cost per cup is
   close to loose leaf. Show the cost-per-cup number from facts.md.

3. **"Who exactly is your first customer and how do you know they want this?"**
   Suggested: name the segment from facts.md and point at slide 4: where we found them, what they said in their
   own words, how many of them there are. If the evidence is thin, say "this is what we would validate in the
   first 30 days with N interviews".

4. **"Water cools in every cup. How does the bag know the difference between 'cooling, stop' and 'I am still brewing'?"**
   Suggested: that is the feature. Each tea has an open temperature and a close temperature (from facts.md, e.g.
   green opens at its brewing temperature and closes a set number of degrees below). The steep stops itself as the
   cup drops below the drinking point, which is exactly when over-steeping starts to taste bitter. Show it in the demo.
   If the numbers are not sourced, say they are a design target, not a measurement.

5. **"Who stops Lipton or Twinings from doing this next year, and what is it you own?"**
   Suggested: name the 3 competitors from facts.md and the real difference. What we own: the brewing profiles
   per tea (the temperature curves) and the app, plus first-mover with specialty tea brands as partners, not as
   rivals. Be honest: a big brand can copy the material; our route is to license it to them, which the business
   slide shows.

## Spare questions, if time

- "Is the polymer food-safe and compostable?" Answer what is known; promise the test, not the result.
- "What does one box cost to make?" Only the facts.md number, with its working.
- "Why a Mendix app for a tea bag?" The Mendix app is the B2B side (brand partners manage brewing profiles per tea); the HTML app is the consumer demo.
