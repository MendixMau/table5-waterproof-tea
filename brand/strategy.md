## 1. Brand platform

| | |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Give tea back its art: the right water, the right moment, the full scent, in every cup. |
| The enemy | **The rush.** A century of convenience that boils everything to 100 degrees, stews the leaf bitter and forgets the bag in the mug. And a world of feeds, alerts and machines that leaves no time to breathe. |
| Belief | In a world of AI and craziness, we go back to what life is about: art, love, scent. Technology should serve the ritual, not replace it. |
| Values | **Patience** (we wait for the water, not the other way round). **Ritual** (a cup is a moment, not a fuel stop). **Harmony** (old craft and new science in one bag). |
| Personality | The quiet host. Calm, warm, exact. Speaks little and means it. Knows 1,200 years of tea history and never lectures. Futuristic in material, timeless in manner. |
| Archetype | Sage with the hands of a Creator. Never the Jester, never the Hero. |

Positioning line (internal, for the jury): *OSME is the tea-tech movement that uses material science to bring back the art the tea bag took away.*

## 2. One-liners and taglines

One-liner = what it is (product clarity). Tagline = how it feels (the movement).

One-liners (under 10 words):
1. **A tea bag that opens only at its perfect temperature.** (9) RECOMMENDED
2. The tea bag that knows when to brew. (8)
3. Opens at the right heat. Closes when it is done. (10, trim to "Opens at the right heat. Closes when done.")
4. Technology that gives tea back its art. (7)
5. Never too hot, never too long, never forgotten. (8)

Taglines:
1. **Every tea has its temperature.** RECOMMENDED
2. The scent of every tea, kept. (strong second: use on pack and for the reseal benefit)
3. Back to the art.
4. Patience, sealed.
5. Breathe in.

Why: the one-liner tells a sales jury the whole product in one breath (opens at temperature implies self-stopping and reuse). The tagline carries respect for each tea's origin (sencha is not Assam), works in any language, and turns a spec into a belief. "The scent of every tea, kept" ties to the name (osmē = scent) and the reseal, so pair them: tagline on slide 1 and the site hero, scent line on pack and the solution slide.

## 3. Manifesto (60 words)

> The world got fast. Feeds, alerts, machines thinking for us.
> So we go back.
> To art: water read by its bubbles, as Lu Yu did in the eighth century.
> To love: a cup made for someone, at its own temperature.
> To scent: the leaf kept whole between infusions.
> Technology serves the ritual, never replaces it.
> Breathe in.
> This is OSME.

Read-aloud time about 25 seconds: fits the pitch close or the solution slide.

## 4. The movement mechanic

- **Members are called Keepers** (keepers of the scent, keepers of the ritual). Subscribers to "Ritual" become Keepers; the welcome box says "You are now a Keeper."
- **The ritual: Three Bubbles.** Once a day, one cup with no screen. Watch the water through Lu Yu's three stages (fish eyes, string of pearls, rolling waves) and take one breath at each. The 3-dot motif is the logo, the ritual and the gesture.
- **Hashtag:** #ThreeBubbles (primary), #EveryTeaHasItsTemperature (campaign).
- **Share format:** a 10-second, phone-down clip of the bag opening in the cup. No faces, no talking, just water and scent.
- **Call to action:** "Take back one cup a day. Become a Keeper." (site button: "Begin the Ritual")
- **Proof of movement for the pitch:** Keeper count, cups kept screen-free, infusions per bag (waste saved).

## 5. Verbal identity

| We use | We never use |
|---|---|
| ritual | hack |
| scent | instant |
| patience | AI-powered |
| temperature | game-changer / revolutionary |
| keep / kept | users / consumers |

Also avoid: "smart tea bag", "app", "disrupt" in customer-facing copy (fine on the jury's market slide only), exclamation marks, emoji.

How to write it:
- **OSME** in capitals in the wordmark, headlines and the pitch. Never "Osmé", "O.S.M.E." or "Osme Tea".
- Short sentences. Present tense. One idea per line. Leave white space; silence is part of the voice.
- Name the tea and its origin (Gyokuro, Darjeeling, Longjing), never "flavours".
- Sensory and exact: degrees, minutes, infusions. Numbers build trust; adjectives do not.

How to say it: **"OZ-may"** (close to Greek osmē, easy in every market). Say it slowly in the pitch, once, with the meaning: "OSME. Greek for scent."
