How it works · Sencha, Japan

Sealed. Open. Sealed.

Boiling water scorches a green tea. An ordinary bag brews at any heat and never stops. An OSME bag waits for its tea’s window, brews inside it, and closes when it is done.

The teas and their cultures

1,200 years of temperature.

Every great tea culture learned to read its water. The tea bag made tea easy and forgot the art. OSME brings the art back, inside the bag.

760

Lu Yu reads the water

China’s Classic of Tea judges water by its bubbles: three stages, three moments.

“The first boil is when the little bubbles like the eye of fishes swim on the surface.”
1908

The tea bag trades art for ease

Thomas Sullivan’s silk bags make tea fast. One temperature, no stop, one cup. Today more than 96% of Britain’s tea comes from a bag.

The movement

The world got fast. We forgot to taste it.

So we go back.

To art: water read by its bubbles, as Lu Yu did in the eighth century.

To love: a cup made for someone, at its own temperature.

To scent: the leaf kept whole between infusions.

Technology serves the ritual, never replaces it.
