# OSME pitch story (storyteller)

Status: draft 1. Grounded in facts.md. truth/history-cultures.md, customers-market.md, business-case.md and brand/strategy.md were still skeletons at writing; every claim that needs them is marked [[NEEDS SOURCE]] with the file it must come from.

## The arc in 5 beats
1. **Roots** (slide 1): for 1,200 years tea was an art. Lu Yu read water by its bubbles; China, Japan and Britain each built a ritual around the cup.
2. **The loss** (slide 2): the tea bag (~1908) traded that art for convenience. Wrong water, a forgotten bag, a bitter cup.
3. **Why now** (slides 4, 5): in a world of AI and noise, people pay for rituals that are theirs; temperature-responsive materials make a bag that knows its tea possible.
4. **The product** (slide 3, live demo): the OSME bag stays sealed until the water reaches its tea's window, brews, and seals itself as it cools.
5. **The movement** (slides 6 to 8): art, love, scent. Ritual subscribers become Keepers; D2C, subscription, premium retail, and the ask that funds the proof.

## The 3-minute script (418 words)

**0:00 to 0:10 · Slide 1 · Hook** (three dots light up as each is named)
Twelve hundred years ago, a man in China could read the heat of water just by looking at it. Fish eyes. A string of pearls. Rolling waves.

**0:10 to 0:30 · Slide 1 · Company**
His name was Lu Yu, and he wrote the first book on tea. From China to Japan to Britain, tea became an art: the right leaf, the right water, the right moment. We are OSME, from the Greek word for scent. We make the tea bag that brings that art back.

**0:30 to 0:55 · Slide 2 · Problem**
Because around 1908, we traded the art for convenience. The tea bag. Boiling water poured on a delicate green tea. A bag forgotten in the cup while you answer one more email. Ten minutes later, it is bitter. In Britain, ninety-seven of every hundred cups now start that way.

**0:55 to 1:40 · Slide 3 · Solution, LIVE DEMO** (pour boiling water into two glass cups)
Let me show you. Two cups, boiling water. On the left, an ordinary bag. On the right, OSME. The OSME bag is waterproof. It waits. Nothing happens. Now the water cools into the window for green tea, and the bag opens. It brews. As the cup cools further, it closes again. The steep stops itself. The ordinary bag is still going, getting bitter. Ours goes back in for a second cup, and a third, with the scent sealed in between.

**1:40 to 2:00 · Slide 4 · Customer evidence**
Who buys this? People who love tea and its rituals, and are tired of ruining it. You see them in reviews and forums, in their own words. They already pay for good leaves, good cups and good kettles, and then lose it all in one forgotten minute. In a world full of AI and noise, they want one quiet moment a day that is theirs, and that works every time.

**2:00 to 2:25 · Slides 5 and 6 · Market and business**
We start where tea is culture: Japan, China and Britain. [N] premium tea buyers, times one box a month, gives us a first market of [€X]. We sell direct, through our web shop and the Ritual subscription, a new box at your door every month, and then through premium retail. Every bag brews several cups, so the cost per cup sits close to loose leaf.

**2:25 to 2:40 · Slide 7 · Competition**
Loose leaf has the art but asks for time and skill. Tea bags have the convenience and lost the art. Only OSME gives you both in one bag.

**2:40 to 3:00 · Slide 8 · The ask**
We are asking [€X] to prove the food-safe material and ship our first Ritual boxes. Technology took the art out of tea. We use it to put it back. Art, love, scent. Every tea has its temperature. Take back one cup a day, and become a Keeper. OSME.

## Three alternative hooks
1. **Te and cha:** "The world has two words for tea: te and cha. Which one you say tells you whether tea reached your country by sea or by land. For a thousand years, tea was a journey. Then it became a paper bag." [[NEEDS SOURCE: truth/history-cultures.md §3]]
2. **The forgotten bag:** "Raise your hand if you have ever forgotten a tea bag in your cup. That bitter sip is what a hundred years of convenience tastes like."
3. **The live pour first:** open on silence, pour boiling water over two bags, and say only: "One of these bags is waiting. In one minute I'll show you what it is waiting for."

## Questions for the team
1. Which tea is in the live demo, and what are its open and close temperatures? The script says "the window for green tea"; it needs the facts.md value. Is the demo physical (two real cups) or the HTML product app on screen next to a real pour?
2. With boiling water the OSME bag must wait for the water to cool to its window. Green at about 80 °C from 100 °C takes minutes in a real cup. Do we fake the cooling in the app, or pick a black tea that opens near boiling so the demo fits in 45 seconds?
3. The ask: amount and use of funds from business-case.md §5. One ask only, per the rubric.
4. Close uses the strategist's call "Take back one cup a day. Become a Keeper." The word "Keeper" is not explained earlier in 3 minutes; OK, or say "Ritual members are Keepers" on slide 6?
5. Pronunciation: script assumes "OZ-may" (brand/strategy.md); the pitcher says it once, slowly, on slide 1.
