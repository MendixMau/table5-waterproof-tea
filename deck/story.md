## The arc in 5 beats
1. **Roots** (slide 1): for 1,200 years tea was an art. Lu Yu read water by its bubbles; China, Japan and Britain each built a ritual around the cup.
2. **The loss** (slide 2): the tea bag (~1908) traded that art for convenience. Wrong water, a forgotten bag, a bitter cup.
3. **Why now** (slides 4, 5): people want the craft and the scent back, and anyone can be a tea person; temperature-responsive materials now make a bag that knows its tea possible. Technology serves the ritual.
4. **The product** (slide 3, live demo): the OSME bag stays sealed until the water reaches its tea's window, brews, and seals itself as it cools.
5. **The movement** (slides 6 to 8): art, love, scent, open to everyone. Ritual members are Keepers; D2C, subscription, premium retail, and the ask that funds the proof.

## The 3-minute script (420 words, about 140 wpm)

**0:00 to 0:10 · Slide 1 · Hook** (three dots light up as each is named)
Twelve hundred years ago, a man in China could read the heat of water just by looking at it. Fish eyes. A string of pearls. Rolling waves.

**0:10 to 0:30 · Slide 1 · Company**
His name was Lu Yu, and he wrote the first book on tea. From China to Japan to Britain, tea became an art: the right leaf, the right water, the right moment. We are OSME. Oz-may. Greek for scent. We make the tea bag that brings that art back to every cup.

**0:30 to 0:55 · Slide 2 · Problem**
Then, around 1908, we traded the art for convenience. The tea bag. Boiling water poured onto a delicate green tea. A bag left in the cup while you laugh with a friend. Ten minutes later, it is bitter. In Britain, ninety-seven of every hundred cups start with a bag.

**0:55 to 1:40 · Slide 3 · Solution, LIVE DEMO** (HTML product app on screen; cooling sped up)
Let me show you. Two cups of Sencha, Japan's everyday green tea. Boiling water, a hundred degrees, and we speed up the cooling for you. On the left, an ordinary bag. It brews at once, far too hot. On the right, OSME. Waterproof. It waits. Nothing happens. Now the water reaches Sencha's window, [seventy-five] degrees, and the bag opens. It brews. As the cup cools, it closes again. The steep stops itself. The ordinary bag keeps going, bitter. Ours is ready for a second cup, and a third, with the scent sealed in between.

**1:40 to 2:00 · Slide 4 · Customer evidence**
Who is this for? Anyone who loves a good cup, and anyone who would love to. Tea drinkers tell us in their own words: they forget the bag, they throw away good leaves after one steep, and the scent is gone by the next day. OSME answers all three. No thermometer, no lesson. Everyone can be a tea person.

**2:00 to 2:25 · Slides 5 and 6 · Market and business**
We start where tea is culture: Japan, China and Britain. [N] premium tea buyers, one box a month, gives us a first market of [€X]. We sell direct: our web shop, and the Ritual subscription, a new box at your door every month. Ritual members are our Keepers. Then premium retail. Every bag brews several cups, so the cost per cup stays close to loose leaf.

**2:25 to 2:40 · Slide 7 · Competition**
Loose leaf has the art but asks for time and skill. Tea bags have the convenience but lost the art. Only OSME gives you both, in one bag.

**2:40 to 3:00 · Slide 8 · The ask**
We are asking [€X] to prove the food-safe material and ship our first Ritual boxes. Convenience took the art out of tea. Technology puts it back. Art, love, scent. Every tea has its temperature. Take back one cup a day, and become a Keeper. OSME.
