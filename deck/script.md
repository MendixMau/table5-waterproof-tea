## Script

**0:00 to 0:26 · Slide 1 · Company**
[Three dots light up as each stage is named; the arc draws under the wordmark.]
Twelve hundred years ago, a man in China read the heat of water at a glance. Fish eyes. A string of pearls. Rolling waves.
His name was Lu Yu. His Classic of Tea made tea an art; Japan and Britain made it a ritual.
We are OSME. Oz-may. Greek for scent. We make the tea bag that brings that art back to every cup.

**0:26 to 0:42 · Slide 2 · Problem**
Around nineteen oh eight, we traded that art for convenience: the tea bag.
Boiling water on green tea. A forgotten bag. Bitter.
More than ninety-six in every hundred cups of tea in Britain are made with a bag.

**0:42 to 1:15 · Slide 3 · Solution, live demo**
[DEMO] The slide already shows the live app with Sencha picked; press D for full screen. Click "Pour boiling water" (or press Space) and leave the time-lapse at 1x, so the cycle fits the words. The jury sees two cups of Sencha side by side with the cooling sped up: on the left an ordinary 1908 bag brews at once and keeps darkening; on the right the OSME bag stays sealed in amber, opens when the water cools to 80 °C, and seals again in jade below 70 °C.
Let me show you. Sencha, Japan's green tea, in boiling water. We speed up the cooling.
On the left, an ordinary bag. It brews at once, far too hot.
On the right, OSME. Waterproof. It waits.
At eighty degrees, it opens and brews. Below seventy, it seals again. The steep stops itself.
The ordinary bag keeps going, bitter. Ours is ready for a second cup, and a third, scent sealed in between.

**1:15 to 1:39 · Slide 4 · Customer**
Curious specialty drinkers: anyone who loves a good cup, and anyone who would love to.
They tell us: I forgot the bag for twenty minutes. I throw good leaves away after one steep. Next day, they are not fresh.
Sixteen percent of British tea drinkers already brew their black tea loose leaf.

**1:39 to 1:53 · Slide 5 · Market**
The world spends seven billion dollars a year on tea bags. Our first markets, Japan, China, Britain and the US: six hundred and forty million dollars. Year three: twenty thousand Keepers, plus tins and retail: nine million dollars.

**1:53 to 2:12 · Slide 6 · Business**
We sell direct: our web shop and the Ritual subscription, twenty-four dollars a month. Ritual members are Keepers. Then premium retail.
A twelve-bag tin is eighteen dollars. Up to three cups a bag: about fifty cents a cup, at a sixty-two percent gross margin.

**2:12 to 2:30 · Slide 7 · Competition**
Loose leaf and gongfu keep the art, but ask time and skill. Tea Forte pyramids look premium, but over-steep and give one cup. Nestlé Special.T needs a hundred and twenty-nine euro machine.
Only OSME: art and ease, in one bag, no machine.

**2:30 to 3:00 · Slide 8 · The ask**
As a tea-tech disruptor, we ask two and a half million dollars on a SAFE at a ten million dollar pre-money valuation, to prove the food-safe membrane and ship the first Ritual boxes.
The principle exists: lab membranes already open and close with temperature. Our R&D makes it food-safe at seventy to ninety degrees.
Every tea has its temperature. Take back one cup a day, and become a Keeper. OSME.
