## Script

**0:00 to 0:27 · Slide 1 · Company: the film · nobody speaks**
Cue to next slide: the film ends on the logo. (A clicks)
[The film plays full screen with its voice: the 1908 hook, China, Japan, the tea bag, OSME opening and sealing, ending "Oz-may! The art of tea!" on the logo. If the screen shows "Press Space to play the film", press Space once. Team stands still and watches the film with the room.]

**0:27 to 0:45 · Slide 2 · Problem · Speaker A**
Cue to next slide: "comes from a bag." (A clicks)
[The 1908 bag drops in, the clock runs, "One cup.", "Scent gone.", "Bitter.", then 96%. About 6 s, no clicks. Do not repeat the film's history or its 1908 card.]
Every great tea culture learned to read its water. The tea bag made tea easy and forgot the art. OSME brings the art back, inside the bag.
Today's bag: one cup, the scent gone, often bitter.
And ninety-six percent of UK tea comes from a bag.

**0:45 to 1:27 · Slide 3 · Solution, live demo · Speaker B**
Cue to next slide: "food-safe at seventy to ninety degrees." (B presses PageDown)
[7 s membrane beat: the bag in layers, sealed, open, sealed. B says the first line over it. Then the live demo, Sencha preselected: B presses Space to pour boiling water. The demo runs on its own; the team explains it. Leave the pauses: the jury watches the dial and the cups.]
Inside every OSME bag is a membrane that opens only in its tea's window, and seals again below it.
Sencha in boiling water, cooling sped up. On the right, an ordinary bag. OSME waits, sealed.
[Pause until the dial reaches 80.]
Eighty degrees: it opens.
[Pause until the dial drops below 70.]
Below seventy, it seals again: the scent kept for a second cup, and a third.
The principle exists: lab membranes already open and close with temperature. Our R&D makes it food-safe at seventy to ninety degrees.

**1:27 to 1:42 · Slide 4 · Customer · Speaker C**
Cue to next slide: "after one steep." (C clicks)
[Title "Everyone can be a tea person." stays on screen, not spoken. The amber wisp draws from each quote into the bag.]
We start with the curious specialty drinker, twenty-five to forty-five, who already pays for good leaves.
One wrote: "I spend way too much on tea to just throw them away after one steep."

**1:42 to 2:03 · Slide 5 · Market · Speaker C**
Cue to next slide: "one point three percent of premium." (C clicks)
[The walk to year 3 is the slide's last row: Ritual + tins + shops. Point at it as you say it.]
Tea bags: a seven billion dollar market. Premium, in our four first markets: six hundred and forty million.
Year three, eight point three million: Ritual subscriptions three and a half, tins three point three, shops one and a half.
Just one point three percent of premium.

**2:03 to 2:17 · Slide 6 · Business · Speaker C**
Cue to next slide: "the monthly Ritual box." (C clicks)
We are cheap for a premium brand, because of our technology: one two dollar bag brews up to three cups.
Sixty-seven cents a cup, half a Tea Forte pyramid. Sold in tins and the monthly Ritual box.

**2:17 to 2:30 · Slide 7 · Competition · Speaker D**
Cue to next slide: "The raise pays for that." (D clicks)
Every way of tea gets something right. Only OSME brings art and ease into one bag.
Where we lose today: the first cup costs more, and the membrane needs certification. The raise pays for that.

**2:30 to 2:50 · Slide 8 · The ask · Speaker D**
Cue to next slide: "Series A." (D clicks once more: logo, "Every tea has its temperature." and the waitlist QR)
[The arc shows how the $2.5 m is spent, not spoken: 35 / 15 / 30 / 15 / 5. "Next: v2, the OSME Capsule" stays on screen, not spoken. The QR stays on screen, not spoken.]
Two and a half million dollars on a SAFE at a ten million dollar pre-money valuation. It pays for a food-safe membrane, its certification, then our launch.
Eighteen months to the membrane. Then the first Ritual boxes ship, and at fifteen thousand subscribers, Series A.
[D clicks: logo and tagline.]
Every tea has its temperature.
