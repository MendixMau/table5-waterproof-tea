The problem
Twelve centuries of ritual, then 1908.
  • One continuous animation, about 9 s, no clicks: the scent thread travels China, Japan, Britain, arrives at the 1908 bag; then the bag cup turns bitter while OSME seals and stays gold; then 96%.
  • China 760: Lu Yu wrote the Cha Jing (Classic of Tea) between 760 and 762 CE; the three boils in Okakura, The Book of Tea (1906), ch. II (truth/history-cultures.md §2).
  • Japan 1500s: chanoyu, as shaped by Takeno Jōō and Sen no Rikyū (1522 to 1591) (truth/history-cultures.md §1 and quote 5).
  • Britain c. 1840: afternoon tea, Anna Maria, Duchess of Bedford (truth/history-cultures.md §1).
  • 1908: Thomas Sullivan marketed silk tea bags "in about 1908" (truth/history-cultures.md §4).
  • 70 °C: OSME Sencha seals again below 70 °C (facts.md, tea windows). The timer (0:00 to 5:40) is an illustration of a forgotten bag, not a data point.
  • 96%: on screen "96% of UK tea is brewed from a bag" (facts.md wording); QMUL 2016: "Over 96 per cent of tea in Britain is now made with teabags" (truth/history-cultures.md §4; facts.md proof points). UKTIA says 97.5%; we use 96%.
